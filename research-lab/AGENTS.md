# Rules for automated and human research contributions

The user authorizes original mathematics publication in this research lab. They also authorized the 2026-10-14 problem-first organization. Neither permission covers private third-party material, headline-solution claims without evidence or unrelated repository changes.

1. Read the actual remote head, `problems/catalog.json`, the relevant problem README, `STATUS.md` and the linked claim/proof/certificate. A prior chat summary is not a theorem. The per-problem next question is the frontier, not an old bootstrap round number.
2. Fix the exact statement, domain, hypotheses, falsifier and acceptance test before work. Keep all groups, a finite group class and an enumerated family distinct.
3. Keep a(G), omega(G), graph coloring and isotropic-cover directions straight. A clique bounds covers from below; an explicit cover bounds them from above. Matching witnesses certify equality only in their stated scope.
4. Record actual executions and failures. At most two bounded experiment workers per daily run, sequential by default, each with a hard limit of at most 35 seconds. This navigation release grants no extra compute, paid API, GPU or local-device access.
5. New finite claims need a complete stated domain and a checker reconstructing key predicates independently. A solver success flag is not enough. Do not redefine acceptance criteria to turn a failure into a pass.
6. Lean must accept the intended statements with the pinned toolchain and an inspected dependency report. No admitted proofs or invented axioms. A checked finite predicate does not formalize the whole group reduction or a census.
7. Keep validity, literature priority, formalization and execution evidence separate. Never mark Lean verification before observing the accepted commit. Suspend downstream claims when a premise fails.
8. Never publish emails, recipient lists, contact ledgers, credentials, personal filesystem paths or unpublished third-party manuscripts. Cite public sources instead of copying full papers. Keep licensing scope explicit.
9. Change only `research-lab/`, its dedicated `.github/workflows/math-research-ci.yml` and the root README's navigation/documentation. Preserve the original sparse-ruler code, results, paper workflow, repository settings and schedules. Existing dated study paths must remain stable unless a separate reviewed migration proves all references and checks survive.
10. Put new work under `problems/<id>/runs/YYYY-MM-DD-short-name/`; use `templates/RUN.md`. Register its proof and source in the catalog, then run `python3 research-lab/tools/catalog.py --write` and `--check`. Generated pages must not drift from metadata.
11. A catalog record is not a new mathematical acceptance test. Add a reviewed verifier to CI when needed; never execute arbitrary catalog strings. Keep documentation checks separate from numerical and Lean verification.
12. Publish one coherent sanitized contribution via a branch/PR or an authorized guarded fast-forward. Read the remote head before writing; no force pushes. On access/safety failure, stop, retain a local release candidate and report the actual barrier. Confirm the resulting commit and CI before claiming success.
13. A scheduled research task is not continuous compute or a separate Work session. CI verifies submitted code; it does not supply an independent research agent. A blocked day needs an honest checkpoint, not a fabricated result or a mandatory commit.
14. Prefer one consequential mathematical step or formalization. After repeated bookkeeping-only units, audit a premise or change tactic. Keep #117 active and other branches parked/retained unless evidence justifies a deliberate pivot. Do not change automation standing instructions or schedules from a routine research run.
