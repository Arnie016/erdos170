# Rank-five three-coordinate alternating maps: 22-orbit finite certificate

**Status:** EXACT_FINITE_CERTIFICATE for the quantified scalar-three-plane domain, plus an ordinary proof of the group-to-graph and orbit reductions. **Literature priority:** PRIORITY_UNCHECKED. **Lean:** UNFORMALIZED.

## Statement and mathematical relevance

For every alternating bilinear map B: F₂⁵ × F₂⁵ → F₂³ of output rank three, its noncommuting graph on the 31 nonzero vectors has chromatic number equal to clique number: χ(B)=ω(B). Here two vectors are joined exactly when B(x,y)≠0.

Combining with the rank-at-most-four all-output census (study 2026-10-08) and the rank-five two-output census (study 2026-10-09), this proves a(G)=ω(G) for every group whose central quotient G/Z(G) is elementary abelian of rank at most five and whose derived subgroup has F₂-dimension at most three. Infinite centers are allowed. Earlier certificates are imported, not re-executed today.

This does not settle any all-group h(n) bound, optimal uniform constant, or the unrestricted rank-five case.

## Reduction and proof of exhaustiveness

If G/Z(G) is elementary abelian, its commutators are central; [g,h]²=[g²,h]=1. Therefore G' is elementary abelian, and commutation is encoded by an alternating bilinear map B on the central quotient. A commuting color class spans an isotropic subspace whose preimage is abelian; conversely an abelian cover colors the noncommuting graph. Hence a(G)=χ(B) and ω(G)=ω(B). This remains true for infinitely many central elements because the quotient determines commutation.

Three independent scalar forms correspond to a three-dimensional subspace U of the 10-dimensional space Alt(F₂⁵). Replacing the three output coordinates by an invertible linear combination does not change B(x,y)=0, so it is enough to classify the scalar three-planes U. Their number is the Gaussian binomial [10 choose 3]₂=6,347,715.

A basis change P in GL(5,2) acts on each scalar form by M ↦ PᵀMP. This relabels graph vertices. GL(5,2) is generated by the five-cycle C on input basis vectors and the transvection T sending e₁ ↦ e₁+e₀: conjugates of T by C yield elementary matrices along a directed five-cycle, and commutators along paths give all E_ij. Elementary matrices generate GL(5,2) over F₂. Thus one complete orbit representative suffices for each isomorphism class of these graphs, without asserting distinct group isomorphism classes.

The C++ code enumerates every reduced-row-echelon basis of a scalar three-plane, marks its GL(5,2) orbit using the two generators, and evaluates one graph per orbit. It found exactly 22 orbits. For each representative the program constructs graph edges two ways: direct 5×5 alternating matrix multiplication and ten-coordinate exterior products. It checks the two graph representations agree, then checks the basis-change action on all 32×32 ordered vector pairs for each generator. An exact clique branch-and-bound uses a proper greedy coloring as an upper bound; exhaustive symmetry-reduced saturation-order coloring finds a legal ω-coloring, verified edge by edge. Lower bound ω≤χ and matching cover upper bound χ≤ω establish equality. Orbit sizes and complete acceptance stdout are in the evidence file.
